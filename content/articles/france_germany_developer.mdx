## Introduction
I live in France, near Strasbourg, and I work in Germany, in Offenburg. Since July 2024 I've been a software developer in the avenit group, and since September 2025 at Vusyon, where I work on web development.

It started with my bachelor's thesis, which I did in Germany. Afterwards I wanted to keep working there: salaries are, honestly, somewhat higher. And I kept living in France, because on the tax side it works in my favour.

This post is less about code and more about what crossing a border for work changes for a developer.

## A border I've been crossing since my studies
Working in Germany didn't come out of nowhere. My path has zigzagged across three countries:

- **Haguenau, France:** a DUT in electrical engineering and industrial computing (2018 to 2019).
- **Offenburg, Germany:** a bachelor in electrical engineering and information technology at the Fachhochschule (2019 to 2021).
- **Neuchâtel, Switzerland:** a bachelor of engineering in computer science and communication systems at the Haute-École ARC (2021 to 2022).

Both of my bachelor's theses were done abroad too: image processing on FPGA at SwissTiming in Switzerland, then a Python test framework at BMG LABTECH in Ortenberg, a few kilometres from Offenburg, where I then worked as a software developer.

## Three languages
French is my mother tongue, my German is at C2 level and my English at C1.

At work I speak German, and I write in German or English. French only comes out with my French colleagues.

## The paperwork
Being a cross-border worker (*travailleur frontalier*, *Grenzgänger*) comes with its own administration: where you pay tax, which health insurance covers you, how pension rights add up across two countries. The rules depend on your situation and change over time, so I won't give advice here.

In practice, there are few constraints. The main one is asking the German side for a tax exemption, with the form for cross-border workers, so that I pay my income tax in France. A cross-border top-up health insurance (*mutuelle*) is also recommended. Beyond that, I find the paperwork manageable.

## The working culture
I really like the working culture. People take the work seriously, and they are very welcoming. For me, it has been a real pleasure.

## Would I recommend it?
Overall, yes. It's worth it: a better salary, few constraints once the tax form is done, and a working culture I enjoy.
